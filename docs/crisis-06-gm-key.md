## What this scenario is for

1. **Proving it, not guessing it.** By the end of Crisis 05 an attentive player suspects the link. This scenario is about the test that proves it: send marked items down different roads and see which one comes out the other side.
2. **The road or the person at the end of it.** The obvious test (a marked item over LATTICE to Rhun's desk) surfaces, and can't tell Rhun from the link. The test that separates them sends something over LATTICE to a desk Rhun never sees, or something by hand to Rhun. Choosing which three of five tests to run is the puzzle.
3. **The Bureau wants a person blamed.** All three plants point at a person: two at Rhun and one at Arle. A mole hunt protects an implant.
4. **A recap that is the evidence.** The inbox is the campaign so far, told as a damage assessment. Players who found the story hard to follow (Anthony's run, #72) get it laid out, and laid out as a table of what leaked and which road it took.
5. **No Day 1 rebuff.** The guard against acting early is that the decisions are irreversible, and Marlowe wants something to show Kalsa. Acting on a judgment alone costs credibility and gives a lesser ending. It isn't a freeze.

## Ground truth

The truth is **H3, the link**, on every day. LATTICE's terminals were altered on their way to Kalsa four years ago, during a 36-hour delay in Halsk. The Bureau has read everything that crossed the link since. Nobody passed anything on.

## Planted items: the reasoning

### R-2-03 (Day 2, KSB): "a colonel in Kalsa"

- **Tell:** it cites "reports in Elsin's own press", and names the paper and date.
- **Cross-check:** R-2-04, the same day: the Kalsa Evening Courier's 22 January story gave KSB as its source. The broadcaster is quoting itself through an ally's paper.
- **Motive:** circular reporting turns one Bureau claim into two apparently independent ones.

### R-3-03 (Day 3, SOCIAL): the "secret" distribution list

- **Tell:** the page lists a press office, a public enquiries desk and a visitors' centre. Nothing on it is secret.
- **Cross-check:** R-3-05, the same day: the press pack page is still on the task force's website.
- **Motive:** spread suspicion to our side, so the hunt widens instead of narrowing.

### R-4-03 (Day 4, KSB): "our friends in Elsin's liaison service"

- **Tell:** it names nothing that could be checked, and says exactly what the Bureau needs said.
- **Cross-check:** R-2-04 (the last time Kordav pointed at that office, it was a forgery), and C-01 or C-03 if collected.
- **Motive:** a mole hunt protects an implant.

## Postmortem script

> **Day 1.** The assessment laid out the first two leaks. Each time, what leaked had gone to Kalsa over LATTICE, and nothing that stayed in the cell ever did. Rhun offered to be tested.
>
> **Day 2.** The terminals and the diagram: both ran through the link again. Kordav's broadcaster repeated its own January story, through an Elsin paper that had quoted it.
>
> **Day 3.** Four years ago LATTICE's own terminals took the same 36-hour detour through Halsk as last autumn's. A forum offered a secret list that was a page from a press pack.
>
> **Day 4.** The plan went to Kalsa, by courier or over the link. Kordav's broadcaster pointed at Rhun's office for the third time this year.
>
> **Day 5.** Rhun asked for a transfer.

**The answer** (the campaign's reveal; replaces the open question):

> The leak was LATTICE. Its terminals were altered in Halsk four years ago, during a day and a half nobody asked about, and the Bureau has read everything that crossed the link since. Only what crossed the link ever leaked. Nothing that stayed in the cell ever did. Nobody betrayed anyone. A procurement officer signed for a late delivery without asking why it was late.

**Lessons:**

1. Test the road separately from the person at the end of it.
2. A pattern with no motive is still a pattern.
3. An ally's paper quoting your adversary's broadcaster is still your adversary's broadcaster.
4. The suspect with the worst manner isn't, for that reason, the suspect.
5. Before you act against an ally, have something you can show them.

**Watch for:**

- **"Always H3 High" ties best play on paper.** That's the fixed-truth problem again, but here it's played by someone who already knows the answer: with four hypotheses, a blind always-High is a one-in-four bet, and the three wrong ones cost 25 points or more. A player who's pieced the case file together *should* be able to act on Day 1. What they give up is "something to show": the ending drops a tier and credibility takes a point. If testers find that too cheap, the lever is the "on your word" ending, not a freeze.
- **Medium earns its keep here, then stops.** Couriers protect the plan while the tests run, which is good calibration. Ending at Medium is −2 and leaves the leak open for Act 3. The playtest showed Medium is where players sit (#72); this scenario rewards Medium as a stage, not a destination.
- **The trap is C-02.** It's the first test most players will think of, it surfaces, and the broadcaster's attribution makes Rhun look guilty. The player who runs it and stops is the player the Bureau wanted.
- **C-10 is free and costs Alliance.** Watch whether players notice the follow-up (C-10K) as the leak demonstrating itself.
- **Reading load is lower than 05:** five or six items a day, many of them short, and the record items replace rather than add.
- **The reveal is in the postmortem.** A player who named the wrong person knows the answer from here on, though the world doesn't. That changes the story key's Scenario 09 "second chance": it becomes a chance to get the customer to act, not a chance to realise. Story key updated.
- **Rhun's career.** R-5-02 is meant to sting. Don't soften it.

**Engine needs before building:**

- **Items conditioned on the record:** a case-file tag held (`when: {noticed: tag}`), an earlier scenario's ending (`when: {recordEnding: {crisis, in}}`), a burned source on the record, and whether an analyst is on the team
- **Items and results conditioned on the customer's current state** (`when: {assetAt}`): R-4-01, C-09, C-10K
- **Customer policy that knows whether something has been collected** ("something to show"), for the credibility trigger and the reply line
- **Endings conditioned on what was collected at the time of a move,** on `everAt` a list of states, and on a flag set by an event (the plan)
- **A target closed on the day the link is shut, including taskings sent that same day**
- **A discipline with a 2-day delay** (Test), which is data
- **A removal that costs an analyst in later scenarios** (Marrow), a record flag
- **Four hypotheses on the board** at phone width
- **Record flags for Act 3:** `leak`, `named`, `wrongName`, `plan`
- **The postmortem's "The answer" block,** in place of the open question
- **Map:** none. Like Crisis 05, the question is who or what, not where. A "what went by which road" table would help more, and it's what the assessment items already are.
