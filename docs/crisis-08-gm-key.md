## What it's for

1. **Crisis 01 again, with the answer reversed.** Then, a routine exercise hid an operation. Now a frightening mobilisation is a show. Every tell from 01 is here, pointing the other way: bridging parked in the open, release dates on the paperwork, hospitals unchanged, a deadline six weeks out.
2. **The adversary adapts.** With the leak open, the Bureau reads each night's assessment and answers it the next morning through a "dispatcher": it confirms H1 or H3 if you hold them, and argues with H2 if you hold that. It wants a High on anything but the truth.
3. **Over-reaction is the trap.** Kordav wins if Elsin calls up (the harvest stops, Kask's coalition strains, the alliance looks alarmist when Kordav goes home on the 25th) or if the task force goes south.
4. **Something to show, again.** Kask decides on the 6th. Halvorsen needs a High on H2 with a result in hand by the Day 4 brief to stop the call-up.

## Postmortem

Script, by day: (1) Kordav called up reservists and said why on television. (2) The bridging was parked beside a reviewing stand, and the reservists' papers had a release date. A video of blood trucks had March on the wall. (3) Kordav set a deadline six weeks away. A forum directive put a Thursday on a Monday. (4) The same forum account contradicted its own directive to point you south. The farm districts were to be released first, as agreed. (5) A parade.

Lessons: Last time's tells can point the other way; read them fresh. A show of force is put where it can be seen. An over-reaction can be what the adversary is after. When a source answers the assessment you sent last night, ask how he read it. Have something to show before you ask an ally to do nothing.
